**Below the flow — Health Score breakdown (visual gauge):**

| Parameter | Weight | Ideal Range | Why This Weight |
|-----------|:---:|:---:|---|
| Soil Moisture | **50%** | 30–60% | Most actionable — farmer can irrigate/drain immediately |
| Temperature | **25%** | 20–35°C | Important but largely uncontrollable by farmer |
| Humidity | **25%** | 40–70% | Affects disease risk but also largely uncontrollable |

**Score algorithm (brief):**
- Inside ideal range → full score (1.0)
- Outside range → linear decrease over 30-unit window → 0.0
- Weighted sum × 100 = Health Score

**Status indicators (visual, color-coded):**
- 🟢 **Good** (75–100) — "Your soil is in optimal condition"
- 🟡 **Moderate** (50–74) — "Monitor closely, consider adjusting irrigation"
- 🟠 **Poor** (25–49) — "Action needed — soil is stressed"
- 🔴 **Critical** (0–24) — "Urgent intervention required"

**Speaker notes:** *"Here's how it works. The sensor node reads soil moisture through a capacitive sensor on analog pin A0 and temperature plus humidity through a DHT11 on digital pin D2. The Arduino's 14-bit ADC gives us 16x more resolution than a standard Arduino Uno. Data is output as JSON every 2 seconds over serial. The dashboard receives this data and computes a health score. Moisture gets 50% weight because it's the most actionable — if the score drops, the farmer can irrigate immediately. The score uses a gradual decay function so small sensor fluctuations don't cause dramatic score changes."*

| DHT11 Sensor | Temperature (±2°C) + Humidity (±5% RH) | **₹40** |

| 10KΩ Resistor | Pull-up for DHT11 data line | Already have |
| Breadboard + Jumper Wires | Prototype circuit | Already have |
| **Total additional cost** | | **₹80** |

**Callout (green accent box):**
> **Why capacitive over resistive?**  
> Resistive sensors have bare metal probes that corrode in 2–8 weeks in wet Indian soil. Capacitive sensors have no exposed metal — they last 6–18 months. They're also less affected by soil salinity (critical for Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan alkaline soils where resistive sensors show 30–50% error).

**Comparison callout (bottom):**
> **₹80** — KrishiSense node  
> **₹15,000–30,000** — Fasal sensor hub  
> **$200–500+ (₹16,000–42,000)** — International IoT agriculture nodes  
> **That's 200–500x cheaper.**

**Speaker notes:** *"Let me break down the ₹80. ₹40 for the soil moisture sensor — it's a capacitive sensor, which means no exposed metal, so it doesn't corrode in wet soil like cheaper resistive sensors. ₹40 for the DHT11 temperature and humidity sensor. The Arduino R4 Minima gives us a 14-bit ADC — that's 16,000+ discrete levels of measurement, far more precise than basic Arduino boards. The total additional cost? ₹80. Compare that to Fasal at ₹15,000, or international solutions at $200-500. We're 200 to 500 times cheaper."*

## SLIDE 20 — Backup: Q&A Reference (Hidden, for your reference)

### Anticipated Questions & Answers

**Q: "How accurate is a ₹40 sensor compared to lab testing?"**
> A: The capacitive sensor gives ±3-5% accuracy uncalibrated, ±1-2% after field calibration. It's not replacing lab testing — it's providing CONTINUOUS relative monitoring between lab tests. If moisture drops 20% since yesterday, you need to irrigate — you don't need lab-grade precision for that decision.

**Q: "How long does the sensor last in the field?"**
> A: Capacitive sensors with conformal coating last 6–18 months in field conditions. Resistive sensors corrode in 2–8 weeks. Our bill of materials accounts for annual sensor replacement at ₹80 — negligible cost.

**Q: "What about connectivity in rural areas?"**
> A: Phase 1 is USB (prototype). Phase 2 is ESP32 Wi-Fi. Phase 3 is LoRa — long-range radio that works 3–8 km with no cellular or Wi-Fi infrastructure. LoRa operates on India's license-free IN865-867 MHz band. Nodes can run on batteries for YEARS.

**Q: "Why would farmers adopt this?"**
> A: We don't go direct to farmers — we go through FPOs, which are trusted community organizations. We train 2–3 "lead farmers" per FPO. The dashboard uses visual RAG (Red/Amber/Green) scoring — no literacy required. And the ROI speaks for itself: ₹800 investment saves ₹10 lakh per FPO per season.

**Q: "How do you compete with CropIn/Fasal which have ₹65 crore+ in funding?"**
> A: Different market. CropIn serves large agribusinesses ($2-5/acre SaaS). Fasal serves horticulture farmers who can afford ₹15K+/year. We serve the 86% of smallholder farmers that neither of them can reach. Also, open-source creates a community growth loop that funded competitors can't replicate — they need margins on hardware, we don't.

**Q: "What about data privacy?"**
> A: All data is anonymized by default. We aggregate at FPO/village level, not individual farmer level. Open-source code means our data handling is fully auditable. We follow the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 guidelines.

**Q: "Can this actually detect nutrient deficiency?"**
> A: Currently we measure moisture, temperature, and humidity — not nutrients directly. But soil moisture is the single most actionable parameter. In Phase 3, we add pH, EC, and NPK sensors (additional ₹1,000) for comprehensive nutrient monitoring. The platform architecture already supports additional sensor types.

**Q: "What's the technical limitation of the prototype?"**
> A: The main limitation is the wired USB connection — the node must be near a laptop. Phase 2 (ESP32) solves this with Wi-Fi. The DHT11 sensor has ±2°C accuracy — sufficient for health scoring but not meteorological research. Phase 3 upgrades to DHT22 (±0.5°C) if needed.
